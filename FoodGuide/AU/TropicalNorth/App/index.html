The Tropical North Philosophy

The Tropical North Haligric Guide is designed for the two great seasons of Australia's top end — the Wet (approximately October through April) and the Dry (May through September). Unlike the four-season model of southern Australia, the Tropics cycle between monsoon abundance and clear, dry vitality. This guide provides the metabolic framework to move with those cycles rather than against them: cooling, hydrating foods during the intense humidity of the Wet; grounding, warming foods during the clear, cooler Dry.

The Calendar Decoder

Schedules are organized by date groups (e.g., 1/8/15/22 ). These represent the Day of the Week (e.g., every Monday). Logic: By repeating the same Tetralty each Monday for a month, you establish a consistent rhythm that allows your body to align with the Tropical North's unique seasonal shifts at a cellular level.

The Tetralty Formula

Every day is a Tetralty: One Grain + One Green + One Protein + One Digestive Seal. This four-fold structure mirrors the four pillars of Haligricity — Thinking, Feeling, Doing and Being — ensuring cellular satiety, elemental balance and metabolic equilibrium without digestive fatigue. . In the Tropical North, we emphasize cooling, light grains like jasmine rice and sago, hydrating greens including water spinach and bok choy, and clean proteins such as tempeh and legumes to navigate the heat and humidity without burdening digestion.

The Seal is a small digestive finisher taken after lunch and dinner. It completes the six Vedic tastes and needs no cooking: a warm Digestive Brew (cumin, coriander, fennel, ginger and lemon) in the damp and spring months, a cooling brew or fresh fruit in peak heat, a Spiced Orchard Anchor of gently stewed fruit in late summer and autumn, and a Mineral Jing Truffle (a date filled with tahini or nuts) in deep winter. No refined sugar — sweetness comes only from fruit, dates and warm spices.

Month 1 · Spring Quarter

The Clear Dry Opening (Wood Phase)

The early Dry Season in the Tropical North is characterised by low humidity, clear skies, and cooling nights. To align your Haligricity here, we focus on grounding the body after the Wet Season's heat and hydration. We shift toward warming grains and mineral-rich vegetables from the local dry-season harvest. The Liver and Lungs are supported to clear accumulated Wet Season dampness.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Kangkong & Tempeh Stir Bowl
Brown Rice (Grain) + Kangkong/Water Morning Glory (Green) + Tempeh (Protein) + CCF Brew (Cumin, Coriander & Fennel) (Seal)
1. Cook Brown Rice. 2. Slice tempeh into strips; marinate in coconut aminos and turmeric for 5 minutes. 3. In a wok over high heat, stir-fry tempeh until golden. 4. Add a bunch of kangkong; toss for 60 seconds until just wilted. 5. Serve over brown rice with a drizzle of lime juice. Kangkong clears Wet Season dampness and cools internal heat.
Pumpkin & Red Lentil Dry Season Bowl
Millet (Grain) + Roasted Pumpkin (Green/Vegetable) + Red Lentils (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Brew (Seal)
1. Cook Millet. 2. Cube and roast pumpkin with cumin and a touch of coconut oil until caramelised. 3. Simmer red lentils with garlic, ginger, and a pinch of turmeric until soft. 4. Layer millet, pumpkin, and lentils. 5. Top with fresh coriander. Pumpkin grounds the body after the Wet Season's dispersive energy.

Month 2 · Spring Quarter

The Peak Dry (Wood Phase)

The Peak Dry brings the Tropical North its most comfortable weather — warm sunny days, cool nights and almost no humidity. This is Wood Phase at its most settled: the body has shed the Wet Season's dampness and can now build strength. We lean on hearty dry-season greens, sweet potato and pumpkin from the market gardens, and grounding legumes to store energy for the heat ahead.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Snake Bean & Chickpea Dry Season Stir
Brown Rice (Grain) + Snake Beans (Green) + Chickpeas (Protein) + CCF Brew (Cumin, Coriander & Fennel) (Seal)
1. Cook Brown Rice. 2. Cut snake beans into 4cm lengths. 3. In a wok, warm chickpeas with garlic, ginger and a pinch of turmeric. 4. Add snake beans and toss over high heat for 3-4 minutes until bright and crisp-tender. 5. Serve over rice with lime and fresh Thai basil. Snake beans thrive in the Dry Season gardens of Darwin and strengthen the Spleen while the body builds reserves.
Roasted Sweet Potato & Red Lentil Bowl
Millet (Grain) + Roasted Sweet Potato (Green/Vegetable) + Red Lentils (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Brew (Seal)
1. Cook Millet. 2. Cube sweet potato; toss with coconut oil and cumin and roast at 200C for 25 minutes. 3. Simmer red lentils with garlic, ginger and turmeric until soft. 4. Layer millet, lentils and sweet potato. 5. Finish with fresh coriander and a squeeze of lime. Sweet potato is a Dry Season staple across the top end — sweet, warming and grounding.

Month 3 · Spring Quarter

The Dry Plateau (Transition)

The Dry Plateau is the calm before the climb. Days stay clear but begin to warm, and the land starts to dry out completely. This is a transition month: we keep the grounding foods of the Dry but begin to introduce lighter, cooling greens so the body is not caught off guard when the Build-Up arrives. The Spleen and Stomach are supported to keep digestion steady through the change.

Mung Bean & Bok Choy Transition Bowl
Jasmine Rice (Grain) + Bok Choy (Green) + Mung Beans (Protein) + CCF Brew (Cumin, Coriander & Fennel) (Seal)

Zucchini & Black-Eye Pea Wild Rice Bowl
Wild Rice (Grain) + Zucchini (Green) + Black-Eye Peas (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Brew (Seal)

Top with fresh coriander and lime juice. Light, hydrating zucchini balances the still-grounding black-eye peas — a true transition plate.

Month 4 · Summer Quarter

The Build-Up Begins (Fire Phase)

The Build-Up is the most energetically intense period of the Tropical North year — temperatures climb, humidity rises, thunderstorms develop. This is Fire Phase: the body needs cooling, hydrating foods to prevent the heat from penetrating too deeply. We emphasize bitter greens to clear Heart heat and light grains that do not add to internal fire.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Bitter Melon & Mung Bean Cooling Bowl
Jasmine Rice (Grain) + Bitter Melon (Green) + Mung Beans (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Cooler (Seal)
1. Cook Jasmine Rice. 2. Slice bitter melon thinly; salt for 10 minutes then rinse to reduce bitterness. 3. Stir-fry bitter melon with garlic and a drop of sesame oil for 3 minutes. 4. Warm pre-cooked mung beans with ginger and coconut aminos. 5. Serve over rice. Bitter melon is the most potent cooling food available in the Tropical North and clears Heart heat during the Build-Up.
Mango & Amaranth Sunrise Bowl
Amaranth (Grain) + Fresh Mango (Green/Fruit) + Chia Seeds (Protein supplement) + Coriander-Seed Cooler (Seal)
1. Cook Amaranth until soft; allow to cool slightly. 2. Dice ripe mango; squeeze fresh lime over it. 3. Layer amaranth with mango and a tablespoon of chia seeds. 4. Top with fresh mint and a drizzle of coconut milk. Cooling, hydrating, and light — suited to the Build-Up's rising heat.

Month 5 · Summer Quarter

The Late Dry Intensity (Fire Phase)

The Late Dry Intensity pushes the heat higher while the air stays dry and dusty. This is full Fire Phase: the Heart works hard and the body loses fluids quickly. We focus on cooling, moistening foods — bitter greens, cucumber, melon and mung beans — and keep grains light so they never add to internal heat. Hydration with every meal is the priority this month.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Watercress & White Bean Cooling Bowl
Millet (Grain) + Watercress (Green) + White Beans (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Cooler (Seal)
1. Cook Millet and let it cool slightly. 2. Wash a large bunch of watercress; leave it fresh and uncooked. 3. Warm white beans with garlic, lemon juice and a pinch of salt. 4. Combine millet and beans, then fold through the watercress so it just wilts. 5. Finish with olive oil and fresh mint. Watercress is one of the most cooling greens available and clears Heart heat during the Late Dry.
Cucumber & Mint Quinoa Salad with Chickpeas
Quinoa (Grain) + Cucumber & Mint (Green) + Chickpeas (Protein) + Coriander-Seed Cooler (Seal)
1. Cook Quinoa and spread it out to cool. 2. Dice two Lebanese cucumbers and chop a handful of mint. 3. Rinse chickpeas and toss with lime juice and a pinch of cumin. 4. Combine everything with a drizzle of olive oil. 5. Serve cool, never chilled straight from the fridge. Cucumber hydrates and cools — the perfect counterweight to the dry intensity of the season.

Month 6 · Summer Quarter

The Pre-Monsoon Surge (Late Fire)

The Pre-Monsoon Surge is the heaviest stretch of the year — building cloud, rising humidity and short, sharp storms that bring little relief. This is late Fire Phase: heat is now joined by dampness, which weighs on the Spleen. We keep meals cooling but add gentle drying foods like adzuki beans and barley-style grains, and keep everything light, fresh and easy to digest.

Month 12 · Winter Quarter

The Return to Dry (Early Wood)

The Return to Dry brings blue skies, falling humidity and the first cool mornings in months. This is Early Wood: energy begins to rise and move outward again after the long inward pull of the Wet. We support the Liver with fresh greens and light, upward-moving grains, while keeping a few warming foods on the table to carry the body smoothly into the new Dry Season.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Kangkong & Chickpea Rising Qi Bowl
Brown Rice (Grain) + Kangkong (Green) + Chickpeas (Protein) + CCF Brew (Cumin, Coriander & Fennel) (Seal)
1. Cook Brown Rice. 2. Warm chickpeas with garlic, ginger and a pinch of turmeric. 3. Add a bunch of kangkong and toss over high heat for 60 seconds. 4. Season with lime juice and coconut aminos. 5. Serve over rice with fresh Thai basil. Fresh greens move Liver Qi upward as the Dry returns and energy begins to rise.
Green Lentil & Greens Stew over Sorghum
Sorghum (Grain) + Kangkong & Bok Choy (Green) + Lentils (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Brew (Seal)
1. Cook Sorghum. 2. Simmer lentils with onion, garlic, ginger and cumin until tender (about 25 minutes). 3. Stir in chopped kangkong and bok choy for the final 3 minutes. 4. Season with lemon juice and black pepper. 5. Serve over sorghum. A light, green stew that bridges the last of the Wet and the first clear days of the Dry.
