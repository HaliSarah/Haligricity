The Temperate South Philosophy

The Temperate South Haligric Guide is designed for the four distinct seasons of Australia's southern states — where Melbourne is famous for having four seasons in one day, Adelaide offers a Mediterranean climate of dry summers and wet winters, Perth combines Mediterranean warmth with Indian Ocean influence, and Tasmania experiences the coldest and wettest conditions in Australia. Despite this diversity, all four share the fundamental four-season structure that the Haligric Tetralty system is built around: the Wood energy of spring, the Fire of summer, the Metal of autumn, and the Water depth of winter.

The Calendar Decoder

Schedules are organized by date groups (e.g., 1/8/15/22 ). These represent the Day of the Week (e.g., every Monday). Logic: By repeating the same Tetralty each Monday for a month, you establish a consistent rhythm that allows your body to align with the Temperate South's clear seasonal shifts at a cellular level.

The Tetralty Formula

Every day is a Tetralty: One Grain + One Green + One Protein + One Digestive Seal. This four-fold structure mirrors the four pillars of Haligricity — Thinking, Feeling, Doing and Being — ensuring cellular satiety, elemental balance and metabolic equilibrium without digestive fatigue. . In the Temperate South, we emphasize the full range of four-season grains — light and sprouted in spring, cooling in summer, hearty in autumn, and warming in winter — alongside the extraordinary seasonal variety of temperate vegetables and the clean proteins that support each season's specific metabolic demands.

The Seal is a small digestive finisher taken after lunch and dinner. It completes the six Vedic tastes and needs no cooking: a warm Digestive Brew (cumin, coriander, fennel, ginger and lemon) in the damp and spring months, a cooling brew or fresh fruit in peak heat, a Spiced Orchard Anchor of gently stewed fruit in late summer and autumn, and a Mineral Jing Truffle (a date filled with tahini or nuts) in deep winter. No refined sugar — sweetness comes only from fruit, dates and warm spices.

Month 1 · Spring Quarter

The Spring Opening (Wood Phase)

Temperate South Spring arrives dramatically after a true winter — longer days, warming soil, and the first flush of spring vegetables from the market gardens of the Yarra Valley, the Adelaide Hills, and the Swan Valley. To anchor your Haligricity here, we focus on the Liver's spring upwelling using local asparagus, baby spinach, peas, and spring herbs. Light, upward-moving foods that reflect the season's Yang expansion.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Asparagus & Chickpea Spring Stir
Brown Rice (Grain) + Asparagus (Green) + Chickpeas (Protein) + CCF Brew (Cumin, Coriander & Fennel) (Seal)
1. Cook Brown Rice. 2. Snap the woody ends from a bunch of local asparagus; cut into 3cm pieces. 3. In a pan, warm chickpeas with two cloves of garlic and a pinch of cumin. 4. Add asparagus to the pan; toss on high heat for 2-3 minutes until bright green and just tender. 5. Serve over brown rice; finish with lemon juice and a sprinkle of nutritional yeast. Asparagus is one of the first local vegetables to appear in the temperate south spring and its upward-moving energy matches the season perfectly.
Broccolini & White Bean Quinoa Bowl
Quinoa (Grain) + Steamed Broccolini (Green) + White Beans (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Brew (Seal)
1. Cook Quinoa. 2. Steam broccolini for 3-4 minutes until bright and just tender. 3. Warm white beans in a pan with garlic, fresh thyme, and a squeeze of lemon. 4. Assemble: quinoa base, white beans, broccolini arranged on top. 5. Drizzle with tahini-lemon sauce and fresh herbs. Broccolini from the Adelaide Hills or Yarra Valley is outstanding in spring — its slight bitterness moves the Liver gently.

Month 2 · Spring Quarter

The Blossom Surge (Wood Phase)

The Blossom Surge fills the southern states with fruit-tree blossom, longer evenings and the fast, green growth of mid-spring. Wood Phase is at full strength, and the Liver's upward energy can easily overshoot into tension or restlessness. We use fresh peas, broad beans, asparagus and leafy greens to keep Qi flowing smoothly, with light sprouted grains and clean proteins to keep the body steady.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Broad Bean & Chickpea Blossom Bowl
Brown Rice (Grain) + Broad Beans & Greens (Green) + Chickpeas (Protein) + CCF Brew (Cumin, Coriander & Fennel) (Seal)
1. Cook Brown Rice. 2. Pod broad beans, blanch for 2 minutes, then slip off the outer skins. 3. Warm chickpeas with garlic, lemon zest and fresh thyme. 4. Add broad beans and a handful of baby spinach; toss until just wilted. 5. Serve over rice with olive oil and mint. Broad beans are a true southern spring delicacy — their fresh, upward energy matches the Blossom Surge.
Pea, Mint & Lentil Stew over Millet
Millet (Grain) + Snap Peas & Mint (Green) + Red Lentils (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Brew (Seal)
1. Cook Millet. 2. Simmer red lentils with garlic, leek and vegetable stock until soft. 3. Add sliced snap peas and fresh peas for the final 3 minutes. 4. Stir through chopped mint and lemon juice. 5. Serve over millet. Sweet spring peas and cooling mint keep the Liver's rising energy smooth and even.

Month 3 · Spring Quarter

The Pre-Summer Warmth (Transition)

The Pre-Summer Warmth brings the first hot days to Adelaide and Perth, while Melbourne and Tasmania swing between warm afternoons and cool changes. This transition month bridges Wood and Fire: the body needs to begin cooling without losing spring's lightness. We introduce early summer produce — zucchini, green beans, strawberries and cherries — while keeping cooked grains as the anchor.

Zucchini & Chickpea Warmth Bowl
Brown Rice (Grain) + Zucchini (Green) + Chickpeas (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Cooler (Seal)

Finish with olive oil and torn basil. The first local zucchini begins the gentle cooling the body needs as summer approaches.
Green Bean & White Bean Stew over Millet
Millet (Grain) + Green Beans (Green) + White Beans (Protein) + Coriander-Seed Cooler (Seal)
1. Cook Millet. 2. Sauté onion and garlic in olive oil; add chopped tomato and simmer 5 minutes. 3. Add trimmed green beans, white beans and fresh thyme with a splash of stock. 4. Simmer 10 minutes until the beans are tender. 5. Serve over millet with basil. A light, Mediterranean-style stew for the warming weeks before summer.

Month 4 · Summer Quarter

The Dry Heat (Fire Phase)

Temperate South summer brings the driest and hottest period of the year — Mediterranean-style heat in Adelaide and Perth, warm but variable heat in Melbourne, and Tasmania's most pleasant season. We shift to cooling, hydrating foods that protect the Heart from excess fire while celebrating the extraordinary summer stone fruits, tomatoes, zucchini, and leafy greens that the temperate summer produces in abundance.

Zucchini & White Bean Summer Bowl
Jasmine Rice (Grain) + Zucchini (Green) + White Beans (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Cooler (Seal)

Peach & Oat Summer Sunrise
Rolled Oats (Grain) + Fresh Peach (Green/Fruit) + Pepitas (Protein supplement) + Coriander-Seed Cooler (Seal)

Add a few fresh mint leaves. White nectarines and peaches from the Adelaide Hills and Mornington Peninsula are among the finest stone fruits in the world — peak season December through February.

Month 5 · Summer Quarter

The Peak Summer (Fire Phase)

The Peak Summer brings the south its fiercest heat — scorching northerly winds in Adelaide and Melbourne, long dry days in Perth and Tasmania's warmest weeks. Fire Phase is at its height: the Heart needs protecting and fluids must be replenished constantly. We build meals around cooling summer abundance — tomatoes, cucumber, stone fruit, watercress and mung beans — with light grains that never add heat.

Tomato, Basil & White Bean Summer Bowl
Brown Rice (Grain) + Tomato & Basil (Green/Vegetable) + White Beans (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Cooler (Seal)

Watercress & Mung Bean Cooling Bowl
Jasmine Rice (Grain) + Watercress (Green) + Mung Beans (Protein) + Coriander-Seed Cooler (Seal)

Layer rice, mung beans and watercress. 5. Finish with lemon juice and fresh mint. The most cooling pairing of the year — ideal for the hottest southern weeks.

Month 6 · Summer Quarter

The Mediterranean Pause (Late Summer)

The Mediterranean Pause settles in as summer's heat eases — warm, golden days, cooler nights and the vineyards and orchards heavy with fruit. This is the Earth season: the Spleen and Stomach take centre stage. We favour sweet, centring foods — corn, pumpkin, eggplant, figs and millet — that steady digestion and bring the body back to balance before autumn.

The Spring Quickening (Early Wood)

The Spring Quickening arrives as late winter loosens its grip — wattle flowers, longer evenings and the first asparagus and spring greens at the markets. This is Early Wood: the Liver begins to stir after winter's deep rest. We ease out of heavy stews toward lighter grains and fresh greens, keeping a little warmth on the plate so the body wakes gradually.

Regional Tetralty Recipes

Broccolini & Red Lentil Quickening Bowl
Millet (Grain) + Broccolini (Green) + Red Lentils (Protein) + CCF Brew (Cumin, Coriander & Fennel) (Seal)
1. Cook Millet. 2. Simmer red lentils with garlic, ginger and a bay leaf until just soft. 3. Steam broccolini for 3-4 minutes until bright. 4. Spoon lentils over millet and lay the broccolini on top. 5. Finish with lemon-tahini dressing. Broccolini's gentle bitterness wakes the Liver as the new spring cycle begins.
Spring Green & Lentil Stew over Quinoa
Quinoa (Grain) + Baby Spinach & Asparagus (Green) + Lentils (Protein) + Fennel & Mint Brew (Seal)
1. Cook Quinoa. 2. Simmer lentils with leek, garlic, thyme and stock until tender. 3. Add chopped asparagus for 3 minutes, then stir through baby spinach until wilted. 4. Season with lemon juice and black pepper. 5. Serve over quinoa with fresh dill. A light, green stew that carries winter's last warmth into spring.
