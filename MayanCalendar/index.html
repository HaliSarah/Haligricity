Haligricity Lens — A Synthesis Note

The Tzolk'in is one of many day-quality systems humanity has built to read the texture of time — alongside the planetary rulers of the Western week, Vedic nakshatras, and the Chinese sexagenary cycle. Haligricity holds this as a lens for reflection, not a replacement for how the day is practically kept. The parallel worth sitting with: 20 day-signs echo roughly the number of days the sun spends passing through each zodiac constellation, and 13 numbers match the 13 constellations the sun actually crosses (the 12 Babylonian signs plus Ophiuchus, the thirteenth sign Western tradition set aside). 20 × 13 = 260 — whether by intention or convergence, the Maya preserved a whole-sky accounting that the West simplified away.

Correlation constant: GMT 584283 (Goodman–Martínez–Thompson), the standard scholarly correlation between the Long Count and the Gregorian calendar. Day-sign and number meanings reflect widely published traditional interpretations; living Maya day-keepers (ajq'ijab') across Guatemala and the Yucatán continue this count today as an unbroken practice. Framed here as a reflective lens, offered with respect for a living tradition.
